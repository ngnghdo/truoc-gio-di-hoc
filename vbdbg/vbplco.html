Understanding Vietnamese Laws, Regulations and Standards

Every country has a system of laws, regulations and standards that governs how its institutions operate, how businesses conduct their activities, and how different industries maintain certain levels of quality and safety. Vietnam is no exception. As you would expect, the country also has an extensive legal and regulatory framework that is made up of different types of legal documents, government regulations and technical standards, that each serve a particular purpose.

Overview of the Vietnamese Legal-Document System

Like most countries, Vietnam operates a structured legal system in which different types of legal documents serve different purposes. Some establish broad legal principles, while others provide more detailed provisions explaining how those principles should be implemented. The foundation of the entire system is the Constitution, which establishes the fundamental principles of the state, the organisation of government, and the basic rights and obligations of citizens.

And below the Constitution, there are different categories of legal documents issued by various state institutions. These include laws and resolutions passed by the National Assembly, ordinances issued by the National Assembly Standing Committee, and decrees and resolutions issued by the Government.

There are also decisions issued by the President and Prime Minister, as well as circulars issued by ministers and heads of ministerial-level agencies. Each of these documents has its own legal authority, scope and purpose.

Main Types of Vietnamese Legal Documents

One of the most important things to understand about Vietnamese legislation is that not every legal document serves the same purpose. Some establish general rules, while others provide detailed implementation requirements or address specific administrative matters.

For example, laws are generally passed by the National Assembly and establish the main legal framework for particular subjects. Government decrees, on the other hand, provide detailed provisions for implementing laws and addressing matters within the Government's authority.

There are also decisions issued by the President, Prime Minister and other competent public authorities. Depending on their issuing authority and legal basis, these documents can approve national strategies, establish programmes, assign responsibilities or address particular administrative and organisational matters.

There are also, ministerial circulars, which are generally used to provide more detailed guidance on matters within the responsibilities of particular ministries and government agencies. And beyond these, the Vietnamese legal system also includes ordinances and resolutions issued by the National Assembly Standing Committee, as well as other resolutions issued by different competent state institutions.

We will explore these different document types in detail, explaining their purposes, issuing authorities, numbering formats and relationships with other legal instruments.

Other Legal and Administrative Documents

Beyond laws, decrees, decisions and circulars, there are several other document types that form part of government and public administration in Vietnam. For example, resolutions can be issued by different state institutions to address legislative, policy or administrative matters within their respective areas of authority. Ordinances, meanwhile, are issued by the National Assembly Standing Committee and form part of the country's formal legal-document system.

There are also directives, which are generally associated with instructions and directions given by competent authorities concerning the implementation of particular policies or administrative responsibilities.

Official communications are also another important category of government documentation. These may include official letters and other forms of correspondence used by government agencies to communicate information, request action or clarify administrative matters.

However, it is important to understand that not every government communication is automatically a legal normative document. The legal status of a document depends on its nature, issuing authority, purpose and the applicable legal requirements.

Vietnamese National Standards and Technical Regulations

Beyond legislation, another important part of Vietnam's regulatory framework is its system of national standards and technical regulations.

These documents establish technical requirements and guidelines for different products, goods, services, production processes and other areas of economic activity. They are particularly important in industries where product quality, safety, environmental protection and technical performance need to be assessed against established requirements.

In Vietnam, two of the most important designations in this area are TCVN and QCVN. TCVN refers to Vietnamese national standards. These provide technical specifications, characteristics and requirements that can be used to assess products, services and processes. They are generally voluntary unless their application is made mandatory through relevant legal requirements.

QCVN, on the other hand, refers to Vietnamese national technical regulations. Unlike national standards, these establish mandatory technical requirements that the relevant products, goods, services or processes must comply with.

Vietnam's technical standards and regulations cover a wide range of industries, including food production, textiles, wood products, construction, transportation, electrical equipment and many others.

Understanding the Difference Between Legal Documents and Technical Standards

Although legal documents and technical standards are closely related, they are not exactly the same thing. Legal documents establish rules, obligations, rights, procedures and responsibilities that apply within the country's legal and administrative system. They can also provide the legal basis for establishing and enforcing technical requirements.

Technical standards, meanwhile, focus more specifically on technical characteristics, specifications and requirements relating to particular products, services, processes or activities.

For example, a government decree may establish general requirements for a particular industry, while a national technical regulation may specify the technical limits or safety requirements that products within that industry must meet. In other words, a technical standard may be voluntary in its normal application, but a relevant legal document can make compliance with particular requirements mandatory.

Understanding Vietnamese Document Numbers and Issuing Authorities

Another important aspect of understanding Vietnamese legislation and technical standards is knowing how to identify individual documents. Most Vietnamese legal documents have specific numbering formats that help distinguish their document type, publication year and issuing authority.

For example, a reference such as 64/2025/QH15 identifies a law passed by the National Assembly during its 15th term. Similarly, a reference such as 78/2025/ND-CP identifies a government decree issued in 2025.

Technical standards also have their own identification systems. A TCVN or QCVN reference generally includes a document number and publication year, making it easier to identify the particular standard or technical regulation being referenced.

The issuing authority is equally important because it helps establish where a document originates and the scope of its authority. For example, the National Assembly is responsible for passing laws, the Government issues decrees, and ministers issue circulars within their respective areas of responsibility.
